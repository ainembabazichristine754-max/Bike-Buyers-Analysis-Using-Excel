Bike Buyers Analysis Using Excel
Project Background

In this project, I analysed customer demographic data to identify factors that influence bicycle purchases.

The objective was to understand which customer groups are more likely to purchase bikes and how characteristics such as income, age, marital status, region, and commute distance affect buying behaviour.

The dashboard was built entirely in Microsoft Excel using Pivot Tables, Pivot Charts, and interactive filters.

Objectives

The analysis aimed to answer the following questions:

Which customer groups are most likely to purchase a bike?
Does income influence bike purchases?
How does age affect purchasing behaviour?
Do married and single customers show different buying patterns?
Does commute distance impact the likelihood of purchasing a bike?
Which regions have the highest number of bike buyers?

Dashboard Components
Income Analysis

This visual compares average income between customers who purchased a bike and those who did not.

Key observation:

Customers who purchased bikes generally had slightly higher average incomes than customers who did not purchase bikes.
Income appears to have some influence on purchasing decisions.
Age Analysis

This section examines bike purchases across different age groups.

Key observation:

Middle-aged customers recorded the highest number of bike purchases.
Older customers were less likely to purchase bikes.
Younger customers represented the smallest purchasing segment.
Marital Status Analysis

This visual compares purchasing behaviour between married and single customers.

Key observation:

Single customers were more likely to purchase bikes than married customers.
Married customers had a higher proportion of non-purchases.
Commute Distance Analysis

This section explores whether commuting habits affect bike purchases.

Key observation:

Customers commuting shorter distances were more likely to purchase bikes.
Purchase rates declined as commute distance increased.
Regional Analysis

This visual compares bike purchases across regions.

Key observation:

The Pacific region recorded a higher proportion of bike buyers.
North America had the largest customer base but also the highest number of non-buyers.
Key Findings
1. Income Influences Bike Purchases

Customers who purchased bikes generally earned more than customers who did not.

This suggests that disposable income may play a role in purchasing decisions.

2. Middle-Aged Customers Are the Main Buyers

The majority of bike purchases came from middle-aged customers.

This group represents the strongest target market for bicycle sales.

3. Single Customers Purchase More Bikes

Single customers showed a higher purchase rate compared to married customers.

This may indicate lifestyle differences affecting buying behaviour.

4. Shorter Commutes Encourage Bike Purchases

Customers travelling shorter distances were more likely to buy bicycles.

This suggests bikes are commonly used for nearby travel and daily commuting.

5. Regional Differences Exist

Bike purchasing behaviour varied across regions, indicating opportunities for region-specific marketing strategies.

Recommendations
For Marketing Teams
Focus campaigns on middle-aged customers.
Target customers with moderate to high incomes.
Promote bicycles as a convenient option for short-distance commuting.
Develop regional marketing strategies based on customer behaviour.
For Sales Teams
Prioritise customer segments with higher purchase rates.
Create promotions tailored to commuters and active lifestyle consumers.
